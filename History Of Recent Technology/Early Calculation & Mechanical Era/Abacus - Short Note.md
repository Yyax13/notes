# The Abacus

The abacus (from Greek *abakos*, also called a counting frame) is a hand-operated calculating system (most versions uses the decimal counting method).

---

## Origin

The abacus was firstly created at Mesopotamia barely 5500 years ago, as an extension of counting with fingers. 

- Created at Mesopotamia.
- Created 5500 years ago.
- Extends the act of counting with fingers.

## How It Works

Between all developed abacus, the most popular way to create it is using two base numbers (5 and 2) to represent decimal numbers, but the older ones uses [[Sexagesimal Numbers]], represented by factors of 5, 2, 3 and 2 for each digit.

The base numbers are small marks (balls, rocks, beads) (e.g.: 5 and 2 means 2 beads above 5 beads, separated by a rectangle or something to hold the 2 beads above).

- Nearly every abacus developed by civilizations used decimal numbers represented by two base numbers (commonly 5 and 2).
- Older abacus used [[Sexagesimal Numbers]] represented by 4 base numbers (commonly 5, 2, 3 and 2).
- The base numbers is the amount of marks (balls, rocks, beads), example: 2 and 5 means 2 marks above 5 marks (separated by something that can hold the 2 higher marks).

## Structure

The abacus is a rectangle with parallel rods, where each rod represent a multiple of ten and hold the counting elements (balls, rocks, beads, etc) there must be able to slide freely.

- The abacus is a rectangle with parallel rods.
- Each rod represent a multiple of ten.
- Each rod holds marks (like balls, rocks or beads).
- There is commonly two base numbers, that are amounts of marks separated, by a piece of the abacus material, that holds the higher marks.
